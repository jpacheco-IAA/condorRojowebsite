01 / THE MISSION

Mars doesn't give second chances.

You are an astronaut on Mars. Establish an outpost, explore the surface, collect scientific samples, and recover five rover data archives while balancing oxygen, energy, and food.

THE CHALLENGE / 01

ONE HOSTILE WORLD. MANY HUMAN PROBLEMS.

Limited oxygen

Your suit uses oxygen during outdoor expeditions. Plan how far you can travel before returning to safety.

03

Scientific fieldwork

05 / THE SCIENCE BEHIND THE GAME

Play the mission. Discover the science.

Every activity in Cóndor Rojo connects a playable decision to scientific questions explored by NASA. The Mars map is stylized; real mission locations are much farther apart.

01

Recover rover knowledge

In the game: copy the archives of Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, Curiosity, and Perseverance, then answer a mission question. In reality: each NASA rover explored a different part of Mars.

NASA source: Opportunity's mission ↗
02

Keep solar power flowing

In the game: clean dust from the solar panel to restore power. NASA connection: during the 2018 Martian dust storm, Opportunity's solar panels received too little sunlight.

NASA source: Dust and solar power ↗
03

Investigate an ancient lakebed

In the game: collect clay and scan sediment layers, learning why a possible biosignature is not proof of life. NASA connection: Perseverance studies the ancient environment of Jezero Crater.

06 / RADIATION & SHIELDING

A solar storm changes everything.

In Cóndor Rojo, space-weather warnings and shelter decisions affect simulated radiation exposure. The scenarios draw on historical NASA observations, with simplified in-game doses.

01 / GCR

Cosmic rays are persistent

High-energy galactic cosmic rays contribute to ongoing radiation exposure and are difficult to shield against completely.

02 / SPE

Solar events can raise the risk

Energetic particles from the Sun can cause sudden increases in exposure. Monitoring and well-protected shelter help astronauts respond.

03 / SHIELD

Protection involves trade-offs

NASA studies shielding materials such as water and polyethylene. In the game, preparing better shelter consumes supplies and energy.

IN CÓNDOR ROJO · FEATURE IN DEVELOPMENT

The game already models cumulative dose, historical solar events, and protection inside the habitat or cave. Historical solar events observed near Earth are used as hypothetical Mars scenarios. Our team is developing a portable shielding-capacity mechanic that can be replenished at the habitat; it is a fictional educational abstraction. Replenishing protection never resets accumulated dose.
